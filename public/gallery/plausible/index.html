Standard-cost simulation

A standard nobody can hit is not a standard. It is a variance generator.

Plausible simulates a season of purchase prices, yields and rates to draw the band a cost could credibly land in. Only 20% of simulated seasons land under this plant's $0.54 corn standard: set a standard outside the P5–P95 band and every period books an unfavourable variance no buyer could have prevented, and here a flat annual average hid a 17.7% Q4 swing.

01 One path — a single plausible season

Set the standard inside the band

A standard cost is a forecast wearing an accounting hat. Test it like one.

  • Seasonal, not annual

    Commodity input costs are simulated by period, because an annual average that looks fine can hide a Q4 spike large enough to swamp the year's margin.

  • The band, not a point

    The output is a P5 to P95 range with the median marked, so setting a standard becomes a decision about risk appetite rather than a single confident guess.

  • Correlated inputs

    Fuel, freight and commodity prices move together, and simulating them independently produces a band far too narrow to be useful.

  • Re-run when the world moves

    The band is recomputed as actuals land, so a standard that has drifted outside it is flagged during the year rather than at the roll.
