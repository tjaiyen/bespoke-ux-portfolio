Working-capital cycle

You pay for the corn in March and get paid for the bag in June.

Conversion follows one dollar from raw-material purchase to cash collected. Holding inventory 74 days and collecting in 38 while paying suppliers in 44 leaves a 68-day gap — and that gap, not the P&L, is what the revolver is actually funding.

01 Cash out — the corn is paid for

Days, not ratios

Profitable companies run out of cash in the gap between paying and being paid.

  • The three legs, separately

    Days inventory, days sales and days payable each move for different reasons, and a single blended cycle number hides which one is actually costing you.

  • Seasonal by nature

    A pack season loads inventory months before revenue arrives, so the cycle is shown period by period rather than as an annual figure nobody experiences.

  • Priced in interest

    Each day of the gap carries a real financing cost at your revolver rate, which turns a days number into a dollar number a decision can be made on.

  • Where the day comes from

    A day of improvement is traced to the customer, supplier or SKU that produced it, because a target with no owner is a target that does not move.
