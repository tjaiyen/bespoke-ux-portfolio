Rates that hold up when the base moves

An indirect rate is a forecast about volume. Most rate models forget that.

  • Pools and bases, explicit

    Each pool carries its own allocation base, so an overhead rate rising because the base shrank is never mistaken for an overhead pool that grew.

  • Forward pricing scenarios

    Model a rate agreement against several business-base cases at once and see which submitted rate you would regret when the volume lands at the low end.

  • Under-absorption early

    Absorption is tracked monthly against the plan, because an unfavourable volume variance found in December is a write-off and in March is a decision.

  • Consistent treatment

    A cost classified as indirect in the pool is not also charged direct to a contract, and the model refuses to let the same dollar be recovered twice.
