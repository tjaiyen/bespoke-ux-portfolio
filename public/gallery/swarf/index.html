The cheapest metal is the metal you never bought

Buy-to-fly is an accounting problem before it is a machining one.

  • Ratio per part number

    Buy-to-fly is measured per part rather than averaged across a plant, because the average hides the handful of parts carrying most of the raw-material spend.

  • Scrap value, recovered

    Chip value depends on whether alloys were segregated at the machine, and the model prices clean segregated turnings against the mixed skip they usually end up in.

  • Near-net trade study

    Forging, additive and plate are compared on total cost — tooling, lead time and machining hours together — rather than on raw-material weight alone.

  • Cutting-tool consumption

    Titanium eats tooling, so tool cost per part is tracked with the metal, which is the pair that actually decides whether a hogged part is worth quoting.
