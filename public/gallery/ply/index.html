Cost that follows the material

Prepreg has a shelf life, an out-time budget and a freezer bill. Most costing systems track none of it.

  • Out-time as a cost

    Every roll carries its remaining out-time, so material scrapped for expiry is booked against the programme that consumed the clock rather than lost in an overhead account.

  • Ply-level traceability

    Each ply records its roll, orientation and operator, which is what turns a cure deviation into a bounded recall instead of a quarantined shipset.

  • Scrap before cure

    Rework caught at ply twelve costs twelve plies of labour; caught after cure it costs the part, the tool time and the slot. The model prices both.

  • Autoclave as a constraint

    Cure slots are scheduled as the scarce resource they are, because a part waiting on an autoclave is a part accruing carrying cost and nothing else.
